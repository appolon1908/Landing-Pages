**Two-sided landing-page business blueprint**

Prepared for Ralph Appolon / Codestra. Planning document; proposed defaults are recommendations, not approved prices, verified market demand, or claims that integrations have been deployed.

**The business: one corporate brand connects customers who need work with providers who need customers.**

Build 30 customer landing pages and 30 matching provider landing pages, supported by a corporate homepage. That is 61 core public pages before support pages, portals, translations, city pages, and seasonal variants. A customer can be a homeowner or a business buying a service. A provider can be an independent technician, a team, or a company.

Recommended starting model: a managed lead marketplace. Customers request help; the platform qualifies the request and matches it to an eligible provider. Make it clear who supplies the service, who provides the quote, and who handles the workmanship guarantee. Initially, a request is not a confirmed appointment. A provider application is not an activated account.

Alternative operating models: a directory is cheaper to operate but provides less control over response and lead outcomes; a full booking marketplace adds scheduling, payments, cancellation handling, and service disputes. The managed lead model is the recommended middle ground for the first release. A separate white-label agency offer can let individual companies use branded versions of the pages, with their own customer data, assets, and billing.

**Corporate homepage outline**

1. Header: brand, Services, How It Works, For Businesses, About, Help, and account access. Make the two audience paths obvious.
2. Hero: a direct statement such as “Find the right service. Grow your service business.” Provide **Find a service** and **Join as a provider**. Place service and city selection on the customer path.
3. Main service groups: home/property, vehicle services, events/learning, and business services. Link to the individual landing pages.
4. Customer journey: describe the request, matching, contact, and quote process. State whether the platform supplies the service or connects independent providers.
5. Provider journey: explain applying, qualifying, setting a service area, receiving opportunities, and reporting outcomes.
6. Trust: authentic reviews, permissioned work photos, verification scope, real coverage, support, and a clearly named company. Describe exactly what a verification badge means.
7. Seasonal feature: one relevant local campaign with a real availability period and truthful terms.
8. Business accounts: a separate CTA for property managers or companies needing multiple services or locations: **Request a business service plan**.
9. Provider recruitment: show the business value and direct visitors to the appropriate provider page. CTA: **Explore provider opportunities**.
10. FAQ and footer: fees, response process, responsibilities, contact details, privacy, service terms, provider terms, lead-credit policy, and accessibility help.

Corporate design direction: restrained colors, generous spacing, readable typography, real local work photos, visible support information, and consistent buttons. Put the business outcome in the headline. Avoid decorating the page with vague futuristic claims or counters that are not backed by data.

**Customer landing-page outline**

Use this order on all 30 customer pages, replacing the service-specific content.

1. A compact header with brand, service area, and a functioning support/contact option.
2. Hero headline stating the problem and location: “AC not cooling in Santo Domingo?”
3. A specific benefit: “Tell us what is happening and request contact from an available local provider.” Do not promise same-day arrival unless operations can support it.
4. One primary CTA: **Request an AC technician**. A secondary **Message us on WhatsApp** is suitable when the inbox is staffed or has a clear after-hours response.
5. A short first form step: service subtype, neighborhood/zone, and urgency. Use postal codes only where useful in that market.
6. Trust close to the CTA: real customer feedback, company identity, and any evidenced verification or coverage claims.
7. Service details: problems handled, exclusions, inspection/visit charges if any, and what the customer receives next.
8. Three steps: describe the job; get matched; discuss a quote and appointment. A request confirmation must not look like a booked visit.
9. Pricing expectations: explain what changes the price. Use a published starting price only when genuine and with clear inclusions. Distinguish a free request from a potentially paid site visit.
10. Proof: relevant completed work and authentic testimonials, with permission to publish.
11. FAQ: response hours, coverage, quotes, provider choice, cancellation, payment, and complaints.
12. Final CTA, concise privacy explanation, and mobile contact controls that do not obscure content.

Customer form fields: service subtype; city/neighborhood; urgency; short job description; optional photo; name; phone/WhatsApp; preferred contact method; optional email; service-contact permission; and separate optional permission for future promotions. Clearly explain that the platform shares the request and contact details with a matched independent provider so that provider can respond. Ask for a full address only when needed for service. Do not require account creation to submit the first request. For tutors, use the parent/guardian as the contact for a minor.

Add conditional questions needed to match each trade: event date and guest count for catering; vehicle type and safe pickup location for towing; subject and preferred schedule for tutoring; appliance type and brand for repair; property/equipment details for HVAC. Collect only details that change qualification, routing, or the quote.

After submission: show a request reference, next action, realistic response hours, and an edit/cancel path. If no provider serves the area, explain this before implying a match. Offer a waitlist or alternative only with clear expectations.

**Provider landing-page outline**

1. Hero headline: “Get HVAC job requests in the areas you serve.”
2. Explain the opportunity: repair, maintenance, or installation inquiries, matched by trade and territory.
3. Primary CTA: **Apply to receive leads**. Secondary CTA: **See how lead matching works**.
4. Suitability: independent technicians, teams, or companies; applicable service categories; coverage; availability; and verification requirements.
5. A clearly labeled fictional example lead, showing area, service, urgency, and scope without displaying a real person's contact data.
6. Qualification definition: actual request, valid contact details, relevant service, covered area, contact permission, and duplicate checks. An unverified inquiry must not be labeled qualified.
7. Lead model: state whether leads are exclusive or shared, what exclusivity means, how long reservations last, and any maximum number of recipients.
8. Commercial terms: when a charge occurs, fee structure, spending caps, pause controls, credits/refunds, and what is not guaranteed.
9. Workflow: apply; complete verification; set preferences and capacity; accept suitable leads; contact customer; update outcome.
10. Provider proof: only actual case studies with clear dates, costs, and outcomes. Before those exist, show the process honestly.
11. FAQ: lead ownership, competition, poor leads, pauses, payment, support, and account closure.
12. Final application CTA and provider terms.

Provider form step one: business/person name, trade, area served, contact name, phone, email, and operating hours. Step two: service radius, supported subtypes, job size, capacity, languages, credentials/insurance where applicable, quote process, and commercial preferences. Collect verification documents securely during onboarding, not through an advertising platform form. Set a real review status and notify applicants of the next step.

**Meta and social-media operating outline**

Set up assets under the correct business owner: a Meta business portfolio, Facebook Page, Instagram professional account, ad account, WhatsApp business identity/number, billing, role-based staff access, and the website data source. Complete the verifications and permissions required for the selected features. White-label client assets should remain owned by the correct client, with delegated access to your team.

Keep customer acquisition and provider recruitment separate. They have different offers, landing pages, qualification questions, and conversion definitions. Recruit service businesses as partners; only describe something as employment when it truly is a job opportunity.

Test three acquisition paths:

1. Facebook/Instagram ad to a service landing page: best suited to explaining a job, capturing details, and showing proof.
2. Meta Instant Form to the CRM: convenient capture; ask enough questions to distinguish a real request and follow up promptly.
3. Facebook/Instagram ad to WhatsApp: useful for a customer who prefers conversation; capture the service and area, then hand off when needed.

Meta documents website forms and Instant Forms, CRM lead retrieval, and CRM feedback through Conversions API. WhatsApp documents ads on Facebook and Instagram that open a business conversation. Feature availability and setup details should be checked in the selected account. [S1–S2]

Suggested campaign naming: country | city | service | customer-or-provider | offer | season | creative. Keep those fields in the CRM alongside campaign, ad, form, and click identifiers where available and permitted. Do not scatter a small pilot budget across 60 independent campaigns.

Creative package for each launched service: one problem/solution image, one short vertical demonstration, one authentic project example or testimonial, and one provider recruitment creative. Match the page headline and CTA to the ad. Use consented real images; before/after claims must be genuine.

Suggested weekly organic schedule: Monday maintenance tip; Tuesday real project; Wednesday seasonal offer; Thursday provider introduction; Friday FAQ; weekend availability update when staffing supports it. Publish for the audience and location served, rather than creating empty accounts for every category. Customer posts point to request pages; provider posts point to applications.

WhatsApp messaging: collect and retain appropriate opt-in, honor opt-outs, offer a clear human escalation path, and use approved templates for business-initiated conversations and messages outside the 24-hour customer-service window. A website form submission does not itself create that WhatsApp reply window. Keep service notifications distinct from optional promotions. [S3]

Measurement: use consistent browser/server event identifiers to prevent duplicate Meta conversions. Treat a click on a WhatsApp button as a click, not proof of a qualified lead or sale. Send appropriate qualification and completed-outcome feedback through the supported integration. Do not send raw job descriptions, uploaded files, or unnecessary personal information to advertising analytics. [S1, S8]

**Lead handling and the two-sided operating workflow**

Customer input channels: web form, Meta lead form, WhatsApp, or call-center entry. Every channel creates or updates one canonical request record. Keep the original source alongside subsequent attribution so repeated contact is not charged as a fresh lead.

Request stages: received → validated → qualified → matched → provider accepted → customer contacted → quoted → appointment confirmed → completed → review requested. Add explicit paths for invalid, duplicate, unserviceable, expired, cancelled, disputed, and no-match requests. A provider application has its own stages: applied → under review → approved → active → paused/suspended.

Matching filters: correct business/tenant, service subtype, supported area, current capacity, language if needed, operating hours, verification status, customer preferences, and commercial eligibility. Never route a lead into a different business or campaign merely because an agent is idle.

Recommended starting routing: reserve one qualified lead for one provider, with a disclosed acceptance period. Before acceptance, show only the job information needed for a decision. Define contact release and charging precisely. Keep contact access locked until one durable accepted assignment and the agreed billing authorization are recorded successfully. Concurrent acceptance and payment retries must not create another assignment, release, or charge. Failed billing must produce a defined retry or cancellation outcome. If the provider declines or the reservation expires before contact access, move to the next eligible provider. After customer details are released, do not silently resell the lead as exclusive. Track each disclosure, notify the customer as appropriate, and apply the published exclusivity/credit policy. Exclusivity describes the platform's distribution of that request; it cannot guarantee that the customer has not contacted other companies independently.

Response target proposal: immediate automated acknowledgment and a human response target within five minutes during staffed pilot hours, only if the team can reliably meet it. Publish actual service hours. If no one responds, alert the operator and follow the documented escalation path. Do not present an automatic acknowledgment as human contact.

The customer record should include request ID, service, geography, source, attribution, permission record/version, validation evidence, assigned provider, timestamps, status, quote/booking references, and outcome. Use separate provider access controls, lead-charge records, contact attempts, and message delivery records. A provider may see its own assigned leads and billing; another provider's customer data remains inaccessible.

Recovery requirements: replaying a webhook must not create a second request or charge; a timeout must not blindly trigger duplicate messages; failed CRM writes must be retried and surfaced; invalid credentials or full provider capacity must produce an operator-visible failure. Confirm restoration from backup before relying on it.

**Revenue, billing, and provider value**

Recommended pilot: free customer requests, with providers paying an agreed price for an accepted qualified exclusive lead. The platform funds customer acquisition from its lead revenue. Explain this commercial role clearly. A lead is an opportunity, not guaranteed revenue. Require genuine provider consent to charges, spending limits, and automatic acceptance if offered.

Optional later products: monthly subscriptions, a managed appointment-setting service, clearly labeled promoted placement, or white-label landing-page/CRM services. A completed-job commission requires reliable booking/payment reporting and dispute handling; do not assume it will work while deals happen off-platform.

Define valid-lead conditions and credit reasons before launch. Proposed credit reasons: demonstrably invalid contact, duplicate already charged within the disclosed window, wrong trade, or outside the agreed territory. A valid customer choosing another provider is not automatically an invalid lead. Publish the evidence and request period for credits.

Provider value formula: expected contribution from one lead = probability of winning a completed job × contribution per completed job, less follow-up cost and lead fee. Illustrative only: a 25% win rate and $200 contribution per job produces $50 expected contribution before the lead price, follow-up cost, and desired profit. That is not a recommended $50 lead price.

Platform contribution = collected lead revenue − customer acquisition spend − refunds/credits − payment fees − messaging/call/support costs − other variable delivery costs. Separately account for fixed staff and infrastructure expenses. Keep country/currency assumptions explicit. Test one pricing model first so results are interpretable.

**Success metrics and experiments**

| Metric | Definition / purpose |
|---|---|
| Customer form conversion | Unique submitted requests ÷ eligible landing-page sessions |
| Qualified-request rate | Requests meeting the published qualification definition ÷ submitted requests |
| Cost per qualified request | Attributed acquisition spend ÷ qualified requests |
| Match rate | Qualified requests with an eligible match ÷ qualified requests |
| Provider acceptance | Accepted offers ÷ valid offers; measure first-offer acceptance separately |
| Contact speed | Time from received request to first real contact; track median and slower tail |
| Booking rate | Confirmed appointments ÷ qualified requests; track cancellations separately |
| Completion rate | Completed jobs ÷ confirmed appointments |
| Provider acquisition cost | Recruitment and onboarding cost ÷ newly activated providers |
| Provider retention | Providers active at the defined follow-up period ÷ cohort activated |
| Lead credit / dispute rate | Credited or disputed charged leads ÷ charged leads |
| Customer repeat rate | Customers returning within the defined window ÷ eligible customer cohort |
| Contribution | Collected revenue minus the costs specified above |

Report by service, city, audience, channel, campaign, season, and cohort. Do not optimize solely for cheap submissions; qualification, provider response, completed jobs, credits, and contribution determine whether the business is working.

Initial experiments: short versus staged customer form; direct request CTA versus quote CTA; website form versus Instant Form versus WhatsApp; real project image versus technician image; general offer versus specific seasonal offer. Test one main change at a time and predefine a sample/decision window appropriate to actual traffic. No universal conversion-rate target is assumed.

**Pilot and expansion sequence**

1. Choose one operating city/service area, the actual brand, language, service hours, and operator. For a Dominican pilot, Spanish-first customer copy with optional English is a reasonable default to validate.
2. Prepare two reusable landing-page templates, corporate homepage, confirmation states, and the provider application flow.
3. Launch the first five pairs: HVAC, plumbing, electrical, home cleaning, and pest control. Replace a category if provider availability or local evidence favors another.
4. Recruit and verify sufficient providers before paid customer acquisition. A planning target is three to five genuinely available providers per category/zone, adjusted to expected volume; this is a capacity proposal, not a market benchmark.
5. Connect CRM, lead validation, permission records, routing, messaging, and operator visibility. Establish lead fees and credits before any charge.
6. Complete the acceptance checks below with real test identities and non-production billing. Then run a capped acquisition pilot with explicit spending ownership.
7. Review cost per qualified request, match rate, response time, provider acceptance, completed work, and contribution. Fix operational failure before increasing volume.
8. Expand to the remaining 25 pairs, then genuine city/language coverage and seasonal variants. Turn on new categories only with providers and support capacity behind them.

**Launch acceptance criteria**

- Every CTA goes to the correct audience, service, and next step. Ads, page copy, offer terms, and forms agree.
- All 30 category records can render a customer and provider page; the pilot pages have complete real content, working routes, and verified contact information.
- Mobile forms, keyboard access, labels, error messages, and confirmation pages are usable. Large assets do not delay the primary task unnecessarily.
- One request arriving twice produces one canonical lead; attribution and consent survive CRM synchronization.
- An out-of-area, unsupported, or capacity-limited request gets an honest outcome. No fake availability or empty provider results are presented as a successful match.
- Provider A cannot access Provider B's leads; campaign-specific call-center permissions remain enforced.
- Charging follows the published acceptance/exclusivity rules. Declines, expirations, duplicates, refunds, and cancellation paths are tested.
- Appointment requests remain separate from confirmed bookings; unavailable slots cannot be confirmed by two concurrent users.
- Customer opt-outs stop the applicable follow-up. WhatsApp service-window and template behavior is verified, with a functioning human handoff.
- Browser/server measurement does not double-count a conversion. CRM qualification and completion events map to supported downstream reporting.
- Failed messages, webhook delivery, lead import, CRM writes, and provider responses produce recoverable queues and operator alerts.
- Support responsibilities, operating hours, privacy notices, provider terms, lead-credit rules, and real business identity are published.
- Release can be rolled back; secrets remain server-side; backup restoration and essential monitoring have been demonstrated.
